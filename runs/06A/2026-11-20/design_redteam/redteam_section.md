## Task 06A

**Build a recoverable compressed attention state across long-context workloads — VERDICT: needs fixes.**

Current raw formula: `S = 0.55 l + 0.30 s + 0.15 e, subject to the attention-state memory envelope.` See [06A §Reward](/workspace/task_design_v2_codex/06_architecture_resource_codesign.md:127). All table payouts are estimates using F above; actual anchors and attainable outcomes are to verify.

### Audit of the task’s own saturation section and kill criteria

The own-section low-bit kill is appropriate and currently the most important early gate. “The selected checkpoint’s causal information paths are unknown” does not defeat data-oblivious quantizers. The 1/4 envelope must be specified for persistent versus transient memory consistently: Verification includes temporary allocations in an envelope introduced as persistent-state bytes. Resolve that ambiguity, include current strong quantizers and chunked recomputation, and run the shared kill tests on their legal implementations. Source: [06A §Saturation resistance](/workspace/task_design_v2_codex/06_architecture_resource_codesign.md:149); experiments: [06A §E2/E3](/workspace/task_design_v2_codex/06_architecture_resource_codesign.md:190). The common protocol critique above also applies.

### Verdict justification

Keep KV compression as a priority feasibility test, not a presumed hard task. Quarter-size bf16 KV has several strong known low-bit attacks, including a Google Research method that does not require checkpoint-specific diagnosis. The fixed operator schema and inconsistent persistent/temporary accounting also need repair. If faithful known quantization is effectively lossless, cut or change the physical envelope only after demonstrating feasible residual headroom.
