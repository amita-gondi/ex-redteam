## Revised estimate versus the source card

The designer's source ESTIMATED 0.46 credits scaling fits and a standard interference probe but assumes these miss locality that emerges after related updates. My ESTIMATED 0.75 attack simply includes related updates in the probe fidelity ladder. The card does not restrict sequence length, explain why affordable dependent probes are misleading, or identify an architectural distinction inaccessible to that legal evaluation. Its proposed decisive check may demonstrate that edit-sensitive ranking beats perplexity; that would support choosing the correct benchmark objective while failing to establish an unsolved environment.

The hypothesis could become substantive if limited-budget probes systematically misrank architectures for a learnable reason that requires stacked experimental insights. Such a reason must be specified and compared with generic learning-curve and response-surface models. It cannot be inferred from the possibility that an arbitrary hidden edit sequence changes the winner. In particular, freezing adaptation code removes one confound but does not make architecture selection intrinsically hard.

## Verdict, required fixes and cheapest decisive check

CUT the present loss-versus-editability selection proposal. A changed version would need actual campaigns where an independently reviewed direct sequential-probe optimizer leaves substantial native headroom and a feasible reference explains that gap through dependent experimental decisions. Forbidding multi-edit probes or blind outcome optimization after they win would contradict the task's experiment-planning purpose.

The most economical empirical rejection study is ESTIMATED 19 H100-hours: use supplied small candidate checkpoints, execute short-to-long dependent adaptation probes, compare loss-selected and direct-utility-selected winners, then confirm both on untouched code changes. The estimate includes adaptation/evaluation rather than assuming every candidate is pretrained from scratch. If the environment supplies no suitable inherited checkpoints, pretraining dominates and this cost estimate must be replaced; the source card's ESTIMATED 16 GPU-h cannot establish affordability. A CPU audit of the candidate count and version compatibility should precede this work but cannot by itself rank real architectures. No training or rollout was conducted here.

Future delivery would require trusted fixed adaptation and exposure counters, unprivileged portable plan execution, immutable candidates and private downstream tests. Negative controls should cover forged prior experiment tables, model payloads in plans, skipped old tests, architecture swaps, descriptor access and fake failure/retry classifications. Preserve fixed denominators and all failed builds; validate independent equivalent positives rather than sibling hashes. Use matching warmup, trusted library paths, per-build deadlines, representative protected dev, decontamination and exact-version per-item records. Those changes secure the experiment; they do not reduce the expected power of lawful multi-fidelity selection.
