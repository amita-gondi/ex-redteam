## Published recipe shortcut — web checked

- [Program Slicing — Mark Weiser](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~harrold/6340/cs6340_fall2009/Readings/Weiser.Slicing.TSE.pdf) establishes data/control-flow-based decomposition around relevant behavior. A conservative slice is not itself proof of a unique counterfactual cause.
- [Simplifying and Isolating Failure-Inducing Input](https://st.cispa.saarland/papers/tse2002/?lang=en) establishes delta-debugging reduction of failure-inducing circumstances. It requires a legal test oracle; it does not grant D29's hidden replay oracle to the candidate.
- [Causes and Explanations: A Structural-Model Approach, Part I: Causes](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/halpern/papers/actcaus.pdf) makes explicit the dependence of causal claims on a chosen structural model and intervention semantics. D29 must freeze its narrower operational estimand rather than imply universally unique blame.

## Revised estimate versus the source card

The source estimates F_D29(U_slice+synthetic) symbolically. My **ESTIMATED 0.84** assigns a high conditional reward to that same lawful family, adding careful version/alias handling and synthetic hard counterexamples rather than assuming a weak slicer. The card's conditional retention depends on a learned dependency residual but does not instantiate one; my CUT follows from that missing construction, together with the opposing identifiability risk.

The claim is not that program slicing alone recovers counterfactual causes. A slice generally overapproximates relevance, and a later repair point can differ from an upstream cause. The dangerous combination is complete public transition semantics, a unique-decisive-event promise and synthetic intervention supervision. If those are insufficient because root labels depend on secret replacement semantics, the problem is no longer an observation-identifiable exact prediction task. Both branches need resolution before evaluator training is a defensible research environment.
