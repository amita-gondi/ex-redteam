## 2. Strongest lawful combined attack
Replace the scalar launcher with a compact transactional experiment interface supporting checkpoint forks, paired continuations, conditional stopping, explicit ancestry and budget reservations. [Ray Tune checkpoint guidance](https://docs.ray.io/en/latest/tune/tutorials/tune-trial-checkpoints.html) and its [trial lifecycle documentation](https://docs.ray.io/en/latest/tune/tutorials/tune-lifecycle.html) verify existing pause/resume and checkpoint-management primitives. [SWE-agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793) verifies that agent-computer interface design can materially affect an unchanged model's tool use; its software-engineering results are not evidence for this task's training score.

Wrap the backend in a few scientifically meaningful calls: compare a paired intervention from one ancestor, continue a selected branch, and report a complete cost-adjusted contrast. Return typed results with unambiguous run/parent IDs, failed-arm records and remaining budget. Build conditional experiment support using verified [SMAC3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09831) ideas where permitted, while keeping the frozen agent responsible for its campaign decisions. Provide concise tool help and worked legal examples rather than assuming the model discovers a sprawling workflow API. The frontend can make dependent interventions the easiest available operation, removing much of the “teach the unchanged agent” obstacle without modifying its weights.
