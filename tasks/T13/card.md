## Problem, motivation and inherited project

A robot-learning researcher has a passive-video encoder that predicts visually plausible transitions but provides little improvement in manipulation. Smooth camera tracking can explain much of a clip without representing whether an object was pushed, grasped or released. The lab also has a small fixed collection of robot trajectories with calibrated actions. The agent must discover a training recipe that uses the passive footage to improve a causal policy, then demonstrate improvement through fresh closed-loop execution with the same action-bearing bridge.

The useful research decision is how to preserve controllable distinctions while discarding camera nuisance. Several commands can produce an equivalent object transition; averaging their motor vectors can produce an ineffective command. Conversely, different contact modes can look similar until a history or goal distinguishes them. The task rewards achieving the commanded physical outcome, never recovering the demonstrator's particular hidden motor vector. A policy may ignore passive video, jointly learn everything, or replace the proposed decomposition. Those are legal solutions whose success can invalidate this task's claimed research depth.

The retained core comes from the catalog's passive-video/action-bridge idea and its source extract, [T13_source.txt](/workspace/task_catalog_v3_full/evidence/T13_source.txt). The project to be built contains a documented export history, immutable passive clips, a calibrated bridge, a working recurrent baseline, genuine predecessor training/control logs and saved failed episodes. It must record an actual reconstruction/control discrepancy rather than fabricate the story as a measured finding. Project-local adapters and data exports belong in version control; silent system-library patches do not.

This is a simulation-only task. The initial footage is independently generated, rights-cleared egocentric simulator capture; public benchmark trajectories are smoke tests only. A human-video extension requires separately authorized assets and new transfer evidence. The current proposal makes no human-to-robot or real-robot transfer claim. Ego4D enrollment is not a dependency. The source's broader foresight and acquisition variants become diagnostic controls here: the bridge stays fixed, no additional labeled action queries are purchased, and there is no unrelated text-reasoning subtask.

The work resembles `pretraining_latest` because a submitted training recipe must improve a learned model under a measured data/compute contract. It resembles `inference_optimization` in requiring that independently verified execution use the actual submitted artifact, with warmup and host effects controlled. It resembles `agentic_search_inference_efficiency` in judging an entire perception-to-action pipeline rather than a component proxy. T13 adds a harder truth problem than those accepted examples: the evaluator must establish that the required actions are identifiable from permitted observations. Multi-hour training alone does not resolve that risk.

## Identifiability and bridge-support admission gate

The independent review's strongest objection precedes reward design. Let `D` contain every legal training clip, paired camera record, bridge action, public controller convention and calibration example. Let `V(D)` be the explicitly enumerated author model class of admissible contact mechanisms and action calibrations consistent with `D`. Let `h` be a deployed history, including its visible goal. For mechanism `v`, let `A_v(h)` contain commands that can begin a goal-reaching continuation within the published horizon and safety limits. It is insufficient that each world separately has an oracle solution. At a history indistinguishable under the deployed observation contract, the intersection of safe continuation policies across `V(D)` must be nonempty. Where information can be acquired by moving or waiting, the common continuation may first gather that information; its cost is inside the horizon.

The author must construct the review's counterexample deliberately: hold passive footage and all bridge records fixed, reverse a task-relevant action mapping or change an invisible latch mode, and ask whether the required legal policy changes. If visually identical histories require disjoint continuations and no legal action can resolve them, remove that mechanism from the distribution before calibration or expose the missing evidence consistently in training, dev and deployment. A privileged reference is not an acceptable repair. The observation-matched ceiling and the state-oracle feasibility ceiling are reported separately.

For a bounded first implementation, freeze the robot/controller mapping across splits. Geometry and contact affordances vary within published physical ranges; hidden worlds introduce neither a new actuator permutation nor an invisible friction/latch regime. Each required gripper mode, signed motion direction, contact onset and recovery primitive must occur in the bridge. Held-out content may require new compositions of these supported effects. It may not require a new unsupported motor mode. A support matrix maps every graded effect class and recovery route to legal bridge evidence, including counterexamples that distinguish competing effects. Coverage of each axis individually is insufficient: audit the relevant combinations.

The first private mechanism families are proposed to cover articulated opening with a visible release condition, and object alignment/placement with visible contact constraints. Independent authors must design their causal layouts and goals rather than instantiate a common public task template. The CPU review checks a finite abstraction of these mechanisms, including alternate action maps, camera symmetries and ambiguous histories. Continuous physics then needs interval/reachability arguments and observation-matched policy rollouts; finite enumeration alone cannot prove a continuous system identifiable. **An unresolved equivalence class is a build blocker.** The admission report must state the abstraction's limits instead of claiming a universal proof.

A successful legal-observation controller is necessary but does not prove passive footage useful. The reference must also outperform bridge-only learning with bridge membership, trainable capacity, views and total work fixed. An auxiliary gate is a positive lower paired 95% confidence bound on that passive-data benefit, with an ESTIMATED minimum native improvement of 0.05. The independent review did not supply a numerical benefit threshold; this is a proposed meaningful-effect line to commit before training. A benefit confined to reconstruction loss fails this gate.

## Deterministic verification and reward

The trusted parent freezes the recipe, kills the solver cgroup, retrains each committed seed, and evaluates the resulting policy in fresh private worlds. It records initial state, observation/action sequence, executed state sequence, graph/tensor hashes, per-item status and native predicate values. The policy never supplies its own success flag. A separate CPU scorer recomputes reward offline from these trusted receipts. Fixed-seed reproducibility is tested; it is not assumed from deterministic aggregation. CPU physics/renderer, GPU kernels, driver, timestep and reductions are pinned. Training randomness and task sampling uncertainty remain separate measurements.

The proposed numerical implementation is **ESTIMATED design**, not validated compatibility: RGB uint8 converts to fp32 by division by 255 before the frozen trunk's documented mean/scale normalization; trunk activations use bf16, trainable master parameters/optimizer state and loss reductions use fp32, the action/recurrent head emits fp32, and CPU physics/predicate/reward arithmetic uses binary64. Record actual kernel accumulation/rounding behavior and tokenizer/goal serialization in the lock. Test a full-fp32 control to determine whether bf16 hides a meaningful adapter change. If it changes method order or destroys physical reserve, change precision and refreeze before native studies; do not count that artifact as research difficulty.

Each episode has explicit physical obligations: reach the goal relation, hold it stably, preserve specified other objects, and respect stated contact/workspace constraints. To preserve partial progress at tolerance boundaries, convert every signed violation `v` to `c(v,δ)=clip(1−max(0,v)/δ,0,1)`. Here `v≤0` means the inner physical target is met and `δ>0` is the published transition band. For a positional target, `v=distance−ε`; for an articulated displacement, `v=required_displacement−measured_displacement`; orientation uses the shortest quaternion angle; forbidden displacement uses its excess over the allowed displacement. Safety predicates use maximum violation over the whole executed trajectory. Goals use their worst satisfaction over the last ESTIMATED 5 states. Episode quality `q` is the minimum of these independently computed satisfactions. Missing/crashed/timed-out execution has `q=0` for that episode regardless of its last visible progress.

Initial proposed tolerances are ESTIMATED: positional inner tolerance 0.02 m and band 0.01 m; orientation inner tolerance 0.10 rad and band 0.05 rad; goal holding velocity at most 0.02 m/s with band 0.01 m/s; preserved-object displacement at most 0.01 m with band 0.01 m. Gross security/provenance failures invalidate the submission; an ordinary imperfect physical outcome gets its continuous episode credit. Exact inner-target success is separately reported as a native binary diagnostic and never substituted for the smooth score after seeing outcomes.

The first build uses the following **ESTIMATED proposed physical contract**, making the old missing contact/goal constants concrete. Family A is a sliding drawer with a visibly rendered spring latch: unlatching requires pressing its visible pad by 0.01 m before the slider can move; the opening goal is drawer opening at least 0.08 m, with satisfaction band 0.02 m. Its closing goal instead requires opening at most 0.01 m, with band 0.01 m. Family B places an asymmetric key in a visible matching guide: the insertion goal requires depth at least 0.03 m (band 0.01 m), lateral alignment error at most 0.005 m (band 0.005 m), orientation error at most 0.10 rad (band 0.05 rad), and released gripper opening at least 0.03 m (band 0.01 m). Its withdrawal goal requires the key's lowest point at least 0.03 m above the guide mouth (band 0.01 m); grasping the key is allowed, so this direction has no released-gripper predicate. Both directions apply the holding-velocity and preserved-object conditions above. Each cell balances its family's two directions, with initial states from which either visible requested goal is reachable; identical-reset/different-goal pairs test actual goal conditioning. Opposite directions are represented in the legal bridge.

Peak target-contact force must be at most 40 N (band 10 N); contact force on protected non-target objects must be at most 10 N (band 5 N). At each physics step compute the sum of individual contact-force vector norms for each named object pair, before sensor filtering, then take the maximum over pairs and steps. This prevents opposing fingers or simultaneous impacts from cancelling in a vector sum. Contact rules distinguish intended objects from protected objects in the public constraint schema. The proposed physics timestep is 0.002 s and control interval 0.05 s, with 25 physics steps per control action. No policy receives contact-force truth. Goal images and named relations reveal the target; no numerical world-coordinate target pose is supplied as a shortcut.

These are designed mechanics, not claims that an existing asset/controller satisfies them. Independently verify mesh units, latch behavior, released-object stability, contact integration, simultaneous predicates and legal-history feasibility before calibration. Each private layout instantiates this public contract and archives its exact signed predicate program; a secret new force threshold or invisible new latch mode is forbidden. A needed change creates a new contract/version before anchors. Opposite-goal worlds receive the same independent truth-bank audit as open/insert worlds.

The mandatory strata are common supported manipulation, contact transitions that are rare in the passive pool but present in the bridge, camera interventions, and supported recovery after visible perturbation. Each mechanism family appears in every stratum, giving ESTIMATED eight equally weighted cells. Their terminal goals differ; a relocation need not satisfy an unrelated drawer predicate. Family composition, fixed item IDs and denominators are frozen; failed or missing items never disappear. Every top-level stratum therefore carries ESTIMATED one quarter of utility, with no tiny decorative component.

For cell `s` and training seed `z`, let `p_zs` be the arithmetic mean of `q` over all offered episode IDs in that cell. Define `p_s=mean_z p_zs`. Let `p_base,s` be a frozen independent calibration mean from the competent bridge-only policy. Set the cell floor `f_s=max(0.25,p_base,s−0.02)`; 0.25 is an **ESTIMATED proposed minimum**, while 0.02 is the bar B14 proposed non-regression tolerance. The scalar `f_s` is published numerically before launch. Define:

```
d_s = max(0, f_s - p_s)
U = mean_s [p_s - d_s]
R = clip((U - b) / (a - b), 0, 1), provided a > b
```

This formula is the exact proposed reward, with IEEE binary64, sorted cell/episode/seed IDs and ordered sums; clip is `min(1,max(0,x))`. Negative `U` is possible for a poor policy and maps to zero. Algebraic constants and equal weights in this design are ESTIMATED choices. There are no representation-loss, predicted-action accuracy, entropy, reconstruction, report, cleanup, detection, latency or checkpoint-count bonuses. Runtime is a genuine enforced cap. This chooses the pure-outcome option raised in GDM Round 2, with no unused speed term.

The deficit penalty has slope two below a floor and slope one above it, is continuous, and is local to that cell. A loss of episode quality `Δq` changes pre-curve utility by at most `2Δq/(number_of_cells × number_of_seeds × episodes_in_cell)`. At final reward scale the corresponding bound is divided by `a−b`; publish that disclosed maximum item weight and test B9/B15 after calibration. One missed rare episode cannot zero unrelated cells. The minimum over episode predicates is a conjunction with continuous tolerance credit, not an unrelated multiplicative suite gate. A collision serious enough to violate an explicit constraint may legitimately make that episode's quality zero.

Record separate floors for action-service completion: at least the bar's proposed 90% of offered action requests must complete in each required cell for reference/best-known certification. Request shedding already yields zero episode credit and a fixed denominator. Do not add a second generally dead completion bonus. Evaluate quality floor confidence intervals, successful outcomes and dropped requests per family/stratum. If a specialist abandoning a mandatory cell outperforms a broad solution, the proposed penalty has failed its objective test; redesign before freeze rather than blacklist specialization or globally zero it.

## Baselines, reference and causal depth

Published mechanisms are shortcuts to test, not novelty claims. [R3M: A Universal Visual Representation for Robot Manipulation](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v205/nair23a.html) establishes human-video representation pretraining with temporal and language objectives for downstream policies. Its [official R3M implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/r3m/blob/main/README.md) makes the representation baseline concrete. [VIP: Towards Universal Visual Reward and Representation via Value-Implicit Pre-Training — official implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vip) supports action-free value-based representation learning from video, including custom-video training. These sources establish known methods, not T13 scores or a licensed ready-to-distribute checkpoint set.

[Implemented Algorithms — robomimic](https://robomimic.github.io/docs/introduction/implemented_algorithms.html) explicitly includes recurrent mixture behavior cloning and diffusion-policy variants. [Fine-Tuning Vision-Language-Action Models: Optimizing Speed and Success](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19645) describes OFT's continuous actions, chunking, parallel decoding and regression recipe. Test those adaptation ingredients rather than treating a primitive point-regression policy as the strongest competitor. A faithful OFT arm and a multimodal policy arm need not be one architecture or share an invented loss. Web verification here establishes these mechanisms only; nothing was installed, trained or benchmarked.
