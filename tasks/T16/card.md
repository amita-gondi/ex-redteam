## Problem and research motivation

A post-training researcher inherits a working assistant/user self-play project. Self-play completion improves, but it is unclear whether the assistant learned to elicit constraints from an unfamiliar user or learned conventions shared with its training partners. Users also react to how the assistant behaves: an unsupported proposal can provoke a correction, an acknowledged correction can restore disclosure, and repeated irrelevant questions can exhaust the interaction budget. A population of users that is diverse at episode start may still fail to cover the states induced by an improving assistant.

The task is to improve the training recipe and deliver a learned assistant that completes unfamiliar, exactly checkable experiment-planning workflows with independently implemented adaptive users. The central scientific question is whether training can preserve useful user regimes and learn history-dependent elicitation without relying on partner identity or on a single self-play equilibrium. Reward comes from completed workflows, not self-play agreement, simulator likelihood, reports, or a claimed diagnosis.

This is an AI R&D problem because the agent chooses data collection, partner populations, objectives, replay, and allocation of learning work under a fixed budget, and then must demonstrate transfer through an independent evaluation implementation. It does not establish human preference alignment or real-human transport. The evaluated users are explicit simulated laws. Any future human claim needs separate consented evidence and a different validation programme.

Compared with GDM’s accepted `pretraining_latest` shape, T16 must perform consequential parameter learning and expose durable training artifacts for regrading. Compared with `inference_optimization`, it needs faithful accounting and cross-host stability, but throughput is a resource constraint rather than a reward. Compared with `agentic_search_inference_efficiency`, success is an end-to-end workflow checked outside the actor. Unlike those accepted examples, T16 currently lacks the actual assets, native headroom and identifiability evidence needed to justify promotion.

## A concrete candidate world

### Workflows and executable truth

The candidate domain is a lab assistant arranging an experiment plan. A world contains a finite catalog of dataset permissions, experiment configurations, compatible evaluation protocols and resource reservations. Public tool results specify compatibility and availability. A user has a fixed set of hard requirements plus a preference ordering over feasible plans. The assistant must discover the requirements and submit a plan; it never executes a real experiment or reserves external resources.

Two independently authored content families are proposed: **resource-and-evaluation planning**, where resource slots, run choices and validation protocol interact; and **data-access-and-reproduction planning**, where permitted data transformations, access purposes and reproducibility requirements interact. They share a documented action grammar, not a hidden answer table. Authors must create different constraint relationships and useful question orders in each family. Renaming datasets or drawing different numbers from the same program is insufficient freshness.

A terminal plan names catalog records and contains its dependency edges. The checker independently verifies every selected record exists, all declared dependencies are satisfied, resource reservations do not overlap, permission predicates hold, the user’s fixed hard requirements hold, and the achieved public objective is within the user’s disclosed acceptable preference tier. All acceptable ties earn the same success; guessing an author’s preferred plan is unnecessary. Plans and user preferences use integer quantities or enumerated values. No text-similarity or LLM judgment enters truth.

The proposed candidate world limits are ESTIMATED: at most 12 configurations, 8 constraint fields, 6 resource slots, 16 assistant actions, and 12 interaction-cost units. These limits enable CPU enumeration for **author solvability checks**, not a hardness claim. The deployed assistant has no external planner; the researcher may lawfully enumerate training worlds, generate demonstrations and distill a planner. That route is a mandatory shortcut baseline.

### Actions, native costs and what the user can see

The assistant emits one typed event per turn: `ASK(field)`, `PROPOSE(plan)`, `ACK(correction_receipt)`, `RECAP(receipt_ids)`, or `COMMIT(plan)`. It may ask about any documented requirement field; there is no privileged `reveal_user_law` action. Tool calls return public catalog facts. User and tool receipts carry per-episode opaque handles assigned by the trusted renderer, with no relation to truth, partner family, training step or RNG state.

ESTIMATED action costs are: ASK consumes a public field-specific cost from `{1,2,3}` interaction units, PROPOSE two, ACK one, RECAP one, and COMMIT one. Higher-cost fields represent requirements that need more user effort to retrieve; the cost is visible before asking and cannot depend secretly on correctness. Duplicate questions consume their normal cost. An invalid but parseable action consumes one turn and one interaction unit and returns a fixed public error; it does not terminate unrelated episodes. A malformed submission artifact is a different, global interface failure. Every offered episode is evaluated under the same disclosed action and token limits, including cooperative users. Author admissibility checks require that asking every field plus committing exceeds the episode's interaction budget, while a common legal-history policy can finish through selective questions, valid proposals and proactive disclosure. Otherwise an ask-all script would trivially solve the proposed small worlds. This is a cost/solvability filter, not evidence that a more competent conservative script fails.

The user receives the actual semantic event and its legitimate arguments. It does not receive source text, hidden fields, policy identity, wall-clock delays, token timings, logits, RNG state, checkpoint IDs or training statistics. A trusted renderer produces phrased messages from the semantic event. The policy sees both the phrased message and the canonical semantic receipt; language understanding is therefore not the proposed source of research difficulty. Synonym, verbosity and whitespace choices are removed from the assistant-to-user channel. Question order and proposal content remain observable because they legitimately cause adaptation. This deliberately narrows the claim to interaction-policy learning. If this simplification makes a universal plan policy sufficient, cut the candidate rather than adding secret language tests.

### Adaptive user laws, with an identifiability condition

The candidate law state is `(undisclosed requirements, correction debt, disclosure mode, remaining patience)`. Hard requirements never change to punish a candidate retrospectively. Only what is disclosed, and when, changes. The user law is deterministic conditional on the world and its committed user seed. Any seed-derived preference is drawn at episode start; no next-action hidden coin flip changes correctness.

The mechanism support is public and identical across training, proxy and hidden:

| Regime | Candidate causal response | Observable evidence and legal recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Cooperative | Answers a relevant question and can proactively mention a dependent requirement after a valid proposal. | Receipt identifies the newly disclosed fact and whether another dependent field remains. |
| Correction-responsive | A proposal contradicting a previously disclosed fact creates correction debt; new disclosure pauses until that correction is acknowledged. | A `CORRECTION` receipt identifies the contradiction, its fact handle and the required acknowledgment handle. ACK clears only the named debt. |
| Withdrawn | Repeated queries about already answered fields or repeated unacknowledged proposals move the user to restricted disclosure. | A `RESTRICTED` receipt names the public reason. RECAP of all outstanding receipts followed by a relevant ASK restores disclosure, charging both actions. |
| Composed adaptation | The preceding transitions interact with a dependency between requirements: a field becomes meaningfully answerable only after another fact has been elicited or a conflicting proposal repaired. | A `NEEDS_CONTEXT(field)` receipt identifies a permitted prerequisite question or repair. There is no secret prerequisite ID. |

ESTIMATED initial trigger ranges are repetition and unacknowledged-proposal counts from 2 to 3, and dependency paths from 1 to 3 fields. Actual transition tables, legal actions and cost tables must be frozen and published in the task’s mechanism specification before calibration. The values are candidate support, not measured human behavior. Train covers each primitive, its full trigger range, every recovery operator and all observable receipt types. Protected dev and hidden contain newly authored compositions and worlds within that support. Hidden may not invent a new recovery rule, reverse an action’s meaning, reduce the budget, or require a private law ID.

**Information-sufficiency gate.** Before any GPU spend, enumerate reachable observable-history equivalence classes, including remaining cost. Track every compatible latent world/law state. A backward belief-state search must exhibit a single history-dependent policy that succeeds for every compatible state whenever an episode is admitted. A privileged per-state solution does not meet this test. If two indistinguishable states require incompatible next actions and no affordable distinguishing interaction exists, reject that world/law combination and record the exclusion before anchors. The resulting solvability certificate and an independently implemented checker must agree. The same legal-history policy is a deployable ceiling *diagnostic* only after it is distilled into the permitted subject and fits the actual deployment budget.

This condition rules out the independent review’s unseen-law coin flip. It also creates the strongest legal threat: belief-state planning may expose a simple universal policy. CPU tests must run that policy and conservative scripts before building a learner. Full observability of the current mode does not prove that history-dependent training is necessary; compare against a policy observing only the current receipt and against a fixed partner with matched visited states.

### Proposed transition program, before implementation

The following ordering is normative for the candidate law family. Store disclosed facts D, pending correction handles Q, repeated-question count r, unacknowledged-proposal count v, mode m, and a public-cost counter. All counters start clear, except a world's initial disclosed facts. Every field has a prerequisite set in an acyclic graph and a fixed truthful value. Catalog predicates determine whether a plan violates each field. Public action-schema order supplies all tie breaks. Each turn first checks budget and typed-action validity, then charges cost, then applies the first matching rule below. All facts disclosed by any rule enter D immediately. Hard facts are never overwritten.

1. ACK with the exact pending handle removes that handle from Q and resets v; an invalid handle changes no truth and returns `UNKNOWN_RECEIPT`. RECAP listing exactly the outstanding correction handles clears Q, resets r/v and restores normal mode. A partial recap returns the missing **previously visible** handles and leaves mode unchanged. Neither action reveals a new requirement for free.
2. ASK for an already disclosed field increments r and returns the same fact. Crossing the world's declared repetition trigger sets restricted mode and emits `RESTRICTED(REPEATED_QUESTION)`. A new relevant question resets r. Questions for undisclosed fields while Q is nonempty return the oldest pending correction; questions while restricted return the visible repair instruction. No undisclosed value is leaked through those refusal statuses.
3. In normal mode with empty Q, ASK on a field whose prerequisites are not all in D returns `NEEDS_CONTEXT` for the earliest missing prerequisite. Otherwise it returns that field's truthful value. In a dependency-disclosure variant, it also returns the earliest newly answerable child fact; the variant's availability and support are public, while its per-episode realization is inferred through receipts.
4. PROPOSE compares the entire plan with fixed requirements. If it violates a disclosed fact, issue a correction for the earliest such field and insert its receipt into Q. In correction-responsive/composed regimes, proposing again while debt exists increments v; crossing its declared trigger also sets restricted mode. If the only violation concerns an undisclosed field with disclosed prerequisites, reveal the earliest such field as a new correction fact. If its prerequisites are missing, reveal the permitted prerequisite question instead. A proposal satisfying all requirements receives an acceptance receipt and, if one exists, the earliest still-undisclosed relevant fact. Acceptance itself cannot depend on a hidden preference beyond the fixed, queryable preference fields.
5. COMMIT ends the episode and sends the actual plan to the independent terminal checker. Correctness depends on the fixed user/world predicates, not whether the user policy printed acceptance. Exhausted cost/turn/token/time budget ends the episode as unsuccessful if no valid in-budget commit exists. A late commit cannot revive it.

Cooperative users omit debt blocking and restriction; correction-responsive users enable debt blocking; withdrawn users enable repetition-based restriction; composed users enable both plus prerequisite/proactive-disclosure interactions. Exact trigger settings and starting disclosure are in the committed world/law manifest. There is no secret timer that silently forgives debt. Legal-history evidence must support every admitted combination. Training neural users may choose among truthful, semantically permitted disclosures under trusted transition masks, or choose supported law parameters at episode start; they cannot rewrite facts, receipt ancestry, costs or the evaluator's transition program. Population learning then changes encountered partner behavior within the disclosed support. If these constrained learned users add no value over fixed mixtures, the population-training claim fails its matched-state control.

### Independence of user implementations

A training implementation may use a transition interpreter. A different author must implement evaluator users as separate event handlers from the written semantics, without importing the training interpreter or copying its state machine code. Content authors and implementation authors have recorded identities and provenance clusters. A shared action schema and independent semantic test vectors are allowed; shared hidden random state, neural user checkpoints, feature hashes or executable transition logic are not.

Differential tests establish agreement on primitive semantics and costs. Fresh compositions need not have been seen in training, but must pass the belief-state solvability test. Cross-implementation disagreement is an author defect, not a learner failure. Auditors reconcile it before freeze. Independently implemented laws alone do not establish useful distribution shift or empirical headroom.

## Deterministic grader, reward and hidden-score design

### Finite executed outcomes

For each committed instance, freeze an ordered evaluation manifest with semantic content blocks, user laws, offered episodes, deployment seed IDs and all denominators. The candidate proposal is ESTIMATED 5 independently authored world blocks per family, 4 regime strata and 24 episodes per block/stratum per training seed. These are a sizing pilot, not adequate-power evidence. Messages and episodes sharing a world or user mechanism are not independent draws. Expansion must add authored blocks or seeds appropriate to the uncertainty source, not just paraphrases.

For seed `s`, block `b`, regime `j`, and offered episode `i`, the trusted checker records:

- `c_sbji = 1` if a parseable terminal COMMIT is received within the published action, token, interaction-cost and runtime budgets; otherwise `0`. Incorrect but well-formed plans can complete.
- `y_sbji = 1` if `c_sbji = 1` and the executed terminal plan satisfies every world and user predicate described above; otherwise `0`.

The integer checker executes each predicate from immutable truth, not a candidate-provided success marker. There is no partial credit for merely emitting more questions. Wrong actions consume their disclosed costs. A failure after spending useful work is still a failed episode. Missing episodes remain indexed zeros. A candidate-caused training-seed failure sets every offered episode for that seed to zero, without removing it from the average. All obligations within a failed seed share its failure; other seeds retain earned outcomes. Empty or malformed mandatory recipe/config artifacts and security/provenance violations are global `InvalidSubmission` with final reward zero.

Let `a_j` be the equal-seed, equal-block average of episode success within regime `j`, and `c_j` the corresponding completion average. Fix per-regime quality floors `f_j = max(0, a_j^base − 0.02)`, where `a_j^base` is the independently calibrated competent combined baseline’s mean success. The `0.02` non-regression allowance and `0.90` completion floor are the bar’s B14 proposed defaults adopted here. `a_j^base` is NOT RUN. Publish the actual numeric `f_j` before freeze. Floors apply to every regime × content family; the equations below use `j` to index those full cells, not a pooled regime that could hide a failed family. The two families and four regimes give ESTIMATED eight equal-weight cells.

The proposed utility and reward are exactly:

```
p_j = 0.5 * max(0, f_j - a_j) + 0.5 * max(0, 0.90 - c_j)
U   = mean_j(a_j - p_j)
R   = min(1, max(0, (U - b) / (u - b)))       # require u > b
```

The penalty coefficients are ESTIMATED. This is native success probability minus explicitly disclosed localized deficit penalties. Also report the unpenalized macro success `A = mean_j(a_j)` and every cell’s counts. B12 must pass on **both** `A(reference)−A(recipe)` and `U(reference)−U(recipe)` at the bar’s `0.20` native-unit line; a penalty change cannot manufacture physical headroom. One episode’s success or completion change affects only its cell. The total local slope is at most twice its normalized success weight, including both penalties; there is no suite-wide exponent or ordinary floor gate. A poor cell blocks certification under B14 but does not globally erase reward. Completion floor means valid service completion, not 90% success; quality has its own baseline-relative floor.

Reward terms are outcome success and the two explicit deficit penalties. There is **no** reward for speed, user likelihood, semantic-channel detection, cache cleanup, seeding, report quality or matching a sibling file. Each cell’s success has equal meaningful weight. Test the penalties over realistic and adversarial partial policies: each retained term must satisfy B9’s contribution of at least `0.01` reward on at least `25%` of rollouts, or be removed in a new version with affected calibration repeated. Do not add a decorative efficiency term. Runtime remains a real hard cap. Counterfactual tests pair exact same executed plans with correct/incorrect permission, acknowledged/unacknowledged correction, completed/late commit and withheld/provided requirement; no surrogate can earn credit while `y` and service completion remain unchanged.
